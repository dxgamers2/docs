FLSRP uses an in-character dollar economy that operates independently of any real-world currency. Every dollar your character earns, spends, or saves exists only within the server and persists across sessions. The economy is player-driven — prices at dealerships and stores are fixed, but the money flowing through the server comes entirely from what players earn through legitimate work, trade, and (for those willing to take the risk) criminal enterprise. Understanding how money moves in FLSRP will help you build a stable financial foundation for your character.

<Tip>
  If you are new to the server, start with civilian jobs before applying for a whitelisted position. Whitelisted roles often have unpaid training periods and irregular shift availability in the early weeks. Having a savings buffer from delivery runs or fishing lets you cover your character's living expenses while you work through the onboarding process.
</Tip>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Earning money">
    There are two main categories of income in FLSRP: job-based earnings and goods-based earnings.

    **Job-based earnings** are the most straightforward. When you work a shift as a taxi driver, miner, garbage collector, or any other civilian job, the server pays you a wage at the end of each completed task or route. Whitelisted job salaries are paid out at regular in-character intervals and reflect the additional responsibility those roles carry.

    **Goods-based earnings** come from selling items you have gathered or produced. Fishers sell their catch at the fish market, farmers sell crops and livestock at agricultural markets, and miners sell extracted ore at the processing facility. Prices fluctuate based on in-game supply, so the same haul can be worth more or less depending on when you sell.

    Other ways to earn money include:

    - Selling vehicles or property to other players through private deals
    - Running a licensed business and generating revenue from customers
    - Providing services to other players, such as legal representation or vehicle repair
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Spending money">
    Your character has plenty of ways to spend the money they earn. Purchases are made at in-world locations — you cannot buy most things through a menu alone; you have to physically go to the store or dealership.

    Common spending categories include:

    **Vehicles** — Purchase cars, motorcycles, trucks, and boats at licensed dealerships around the map. Vehicle prices range from a few thousand dollars for a used economy car to hundreds of thousands for high-end sports cars and custom builds.

    **Properties** — Rent or buy residential and commercial properties. Owning a property gives your character a permanent home location and, in the case of businesses, a source of ongoing revenue.

    **Clothing and accessories** — Visit clothing stores to update your character's appearance. New outfits, hats, and accessories are all available at standard retail prices.

    **Food and consumables** — Purchase food and drinks at convenience stores and restaurants. Some consumables affect your character's health or stamina in the short term.

    **Vehicle modifications** — Take your car to a mechanic or mod shop to upgrade performance, change paint, add custom plates, and more. Modifications are charged at market rate and vary by shop.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Owning a business">
    Established players can purchase or lease a business property to generate income and create roleplay opportunities for other players. Business ownership is a mid-to-late-stage goal that requires significant upfront capital, but it provides a passive revenue stream once operational.

    To start a business:

    1. Identify an available commercial property on the business listings board (found in City Hall or via the phone).
    2. Pay the purchase or lease fee to claim the property.
    3. Stock the business with inventory, which you source through suppliers or by producing goods yourself.
    4. Open the business during your character's active hours and serve other players who walk in.

    Businesses can range from convenience stores and clothing boutiques to auto shops, restaurants, and underground operations. All businesses must be operated in character and in compliance with server rules. Staff can be hired from other players, creating employment RP and additional income for both parties.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Banking">
    Your character has access to a personal bank account that you manage through ATMs and bank branches located around Los Santos and Blaine County. Cash carried on your person can be lost if your character is robbed or dies, so depositing your earnings regularly is strongly advised.

    **Depositing cash** — Walk up to any ATM or enter a bank branch and interact with the terminal to deposit cash from your wallet into your account. Deposited money is secure and cannot be taken by other players.

    **Withdrawing cash** — Withdraw any amount up to your current balance at any ATM. You need cash on hand for purchases at certain locations that do not accept card payments.

    **Checking your balance** — Your bank balance and cash on hand are both visible in the character information panel. Keep an eye on both figures — running out of cash with no ATM nearby can leave your character unable to make purchases in the moment.

    **Transfers** — You can transfer money to another player's bank account directly through the banking terminal. Use this for splitting bills, paying rent, completing private sales, or settling debts between characters.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  Money obtained through exploits, duplication bugs, or any other unintended game mechanic must be reported to staff immediately. Do not spend it. Any funds identified as illegitimately obtained will be removed from your character's account, and players who knowingly exploit the economy risk disciplinary action up to and including a permanent ban.
</Warning>
