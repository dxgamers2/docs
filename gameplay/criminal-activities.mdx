Criminal roleplay is one of the most dynamic and story-rich parts of FLSRP. Running drugs through the city, staging a store robbery, or dealing in illegal weapons can produce some of the server's most memorable moments — but only when it is done properly. Every criminal action must be grounded in genuine roleplay. The risk is real: police respond, arrests happen, contraband is seized, and characters go to jail. That friction is what makes criminal RP rewarding when it succeeds.

<Warning>
  Criminal activities exist to create compelling RP scenarios, not to grief or harass other players. Using criminal mechanics to repeatedly target the same player without narrative justification, to spawn-kill, or to exploit the system for easy money with no roleplay is a violation of server rules and will result in disciplinary action. If your goal is to have fun at someone else's expense rather than to tell a story, you are doing it wrong.
</Warning>

## Available criminal activities

FLSRP supports a range of criminal operations, each with its own mechanics, supply chains, and risk profile.

**Drug manufacturing and running** — Source raw materials, process them at a hidden lab location, and distribute the finished product to buyers around the city. Each stage of the supply chain is an opportunity for police interception. Finished product carried on your person or in your vehicle is contraband and can be seized on arrest or lost on death.

**Robbery** — Rob convenience stores, gas stations, or other retail locations for a cash payout. Robberies trigger a police dispatch, and staying long enough to empty the register increases both your take and your exposure time. Player-on-player robbery is also permitted under the right roleplay conditions — see the robbery scenario example below for what proper robbery RP looks like.

**Carjacking** — Forcibly take a vehicle from another player or steal an unoccupied vehicle. Stolen vehicles can be sold to a chop shop for cash. Keep in mind that driving a stolen vehicle around the city increases your chances of a police stop and a flagged plate check.

**Illegal arms dealing** — Source unlicensed weapons and sell them to other players. The weapons market carries significant legal risk — firearms charges add substantial jail time if you are caught. Both buyer and seller take on risk in any illegal weapons transaction.

## Consequences

Criminal activity in FLSRP is balanced by a real consequences system. Nothing is without risk, and the stakes are part of what makes criminal RP meaningful.

- **Police response** — Most criminal activities trigger a live dispatch to LSPD. Officers respond in character, meaning the quality and speed of the response depends on who is on duty and how the call is reported. A fast robbery with a clean exit is very different from a standoff that draws half the department.
- **Arrest and jail time** — If your character is caught and arrested, they are processed and sent to jail for an in-character period based on the charges. Time in jail is served in full; you cannot simply log off and return later to skip it.
- **Loss of contraband** — Any drugs, illegal weapons, or stolen goods on your character or in your vehicle at the time of arrest are seized permanently. You do not get them back.
- **Death penalties** — If your character is killed during a criminal operation, you lose any contraband on your person. The New Life Rule also applies — your character forgets the events leading to their death, which ends any active criminal scenario for that character.

<Note>
  FLSRP follows a cop-to-criminal ratio guideline to keep criminal RP fair and functional. When very few police officers are online, large-scale criminal operations such as bank heists or coordinated gang activity may be restricted or require staff approval before proceeding. Check the active officer count before initiating a major criminal scenario, and contact staff if you are unsure whether the current conditions allow for the activity you have planned.
</Note>

## How proper criminal RP looks: a robbery example

The following is an example of a well-executed store robbery scenario that demonstrates the standard expected of all criminal players on FLSRP.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Establish your character's motivation">
    Before you even approach the store, know why your character is doing this. Are they desperate for cash after a business failure? Paying off a debt to a gang? Testing the waters before a larger job? Your motivation does not need to be said out loud, but it should inform how your character acts throughout the scenario.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Case the location in character">
    Drive past the store, go inside as a customer, or have a conversation with a nearby NPC to establish presence. This step is optional for small jobs but adds depth to the RP and gives any observant players (including undercover officers) something to react to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Initiate the robbery with clear roleplay">
    Enter the store and initiate the robbery through voice or text RP before using any mechanics. Give the clerk — played by an NPC or another player — a clear, in-character command: what you want, what will happen if they do not comply, and a reasonable window to respond. Do not immediately use a weapon before any RP has occurred.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give other players time to react">
    If other players are present in the store, acknowledge them in character. A realistic robber does not ignore three witnesses standing in the cereal aisle. Direct them to the floor, keep an eye on them, and maintain the scenario as a living scene rather than a transaction with an NPC.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute your exit and accept the outcome">
    Leave the scene with your take. If police arrive before you are clear, decide in character whether your character runs, surrenders, or negotiates — not based on what is mechanically advantageous, but based on what your character would actually do. If you are caught, roleplay the arrest fully and accept the consequences. A good arrest scene is just as valuable as a successful escape.
  </Step>
</Steps>
